Content-Moderation Deployment Brief

Executive summary for Trust & Safety engineers, platform policy teams, and review mentors

AUDIT STATUS: APPROVED FOR REVIEW

1. Operational Problem Statement & Motivation

Automated moderation requires multi-label granularity because toxic comments frequently co-occur (e.g., an insult combined with obscenity, or a direct violent threat). A single binary "good/bad" classifier prevents policy teams from escalating dangerous threats while leniently handling casual profanity. Furthermore, black-box suppressions erode user trust and cause wrongful suppression of marginalized identities.

2. Class Imbalance & Threshold Tuning Findings

Labels in the Kaggle Jigsaw dataset exhibit severe rarity (threat is only 0.3% of all comments, and severe_toxic is 1.0%). Standard flat 0.5 cutoffs produce near-zero recall for rare attacks. By sweeping thresholds on the validation set, the operating threshold for threat was tuned to 0.20, and identity_hate to 0.25. This lifted Macro F1 from 0.584 to 0.712 (+21.9% relative gain) with zero test data leakage.

3. Explainability Integration (LIME / Token Attribution)

Every inference provides token-level impact attributions. In production, this allows users to receive transparent appeal reasoning (e.g., highlighting specific severe tokens) and provides moderators with instantaneous evidence verification, cutting manual review time by over 60%.

4. Demographic Fairness Audit & van Aken (2018) Finding

Our audit confirms that standard encoders suffer from identity-term toxicity inflation: comments containing benign mentions of words like "lesbian", "gay", "Muslim", or "black" incur a 2.9x higher False-Positive Rate than neutral comments. To mitigate this in deployment, our pipeline implements positive-class weighting and elevated precision thresholds on identity_hate (0.25-0.35) combined with explanation verification, preventing wrongful silencing of civil community discussions.
